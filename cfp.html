Call for Papers

Controllable Data Memorization for Trustworthy AI

Topics of Interest

We solicit contributions on Controllable Data Memorization for Trustworthy AI. Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

Understanding and measuring memorization

  • Memorization, generalization, and training-data influence.
  • Attribution, provenance, localization, and causal tracing of information learned from data.
  • Measurement and auditing of memorized information in parameters, representations, and model outputs.
  • Privacy leakage, extraction, and reconstruction as consequences of unintended memorization.

Limiting and preventing undesired memorization

  • Privacy-preserving learning and differential privacy for limiting sensitive information retention or exposure.
  • Prevention of undesirable influence from poisoned, corrupted, biased, or adversarial data.
  • Robust learning methods for limiting spurious, corrupted, or adversarial data influence.
  • Control of information acquisition during training, fine-tuning, and adaptation.

Modifying and removing memorized information

  • Machine unlearning for removing the influence of examples, users, features, concepts, or knowledge.
  • Model editing and targeted correction of learned knowledge or behavior.
  • Selective forgetting and removal of outdated, harmful, or unwanted information.
  • Efficient modification of learned information under repeated model updates.

Preserving and managing useful memorization

  • Retention of useful knowledge and capabilities during model updates.
  • Continual and lifelong learning with controlled retention, adaptation, and forgetting.
  • Mitigation of catastrophic forgetting and interference.
  • Balancing preservation of desired information with modification of targeted information.

Verification and limits of memorization control

  • Definitions, metrics, and evaluation protocols for memorization and its control.
  • Auditing and verification of privacy, deletion, editing, retention, and robustness properties.
  • Theoretical guarantees for controlling, modifying, or removing learned information.
  • Tradeoffs, lower bounds, impossibility results, and failure modes of memorization control.
