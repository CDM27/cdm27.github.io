Controllable Data Memorization for Trustworthy AI

On this page
Topics of InterestImportant DatesPaper SubmissionPublication

CDM 2027 invites submissions on controllable data memorization for trustworthy AI.

Topics of Interest

The topics below illustrate the scope of the workshop. Contributions may span multiple themes or address related questions.

Understanding What Models Retain

  • Mechanisms of memorization and its relationship to generalization and training-data influence.
  • Attribution, provenance, localization, and causal tracing of information learned from data.
  • Measurement and auditing of memorization in model parameters, representations, and outputs.
  • Exposure, extraction, and reconstruction of memorized training information.

Limiting Unwanted Memorization and Data Influence

  • Selective control over what models acquire and retain during training, fine-tuning, and adaptation.
  • Differential privacy to bound the influence of individual training examples or users, and other methods for limiting the retention or exposure of sensitive information.
  • Robust learning to limit the influence of biased, corrupted, poisoned, or adversarial training data and reduce reliance on spurious correlations.

Unlearning and Model Editing

  • Machine unlearning to remove the influence of specific examples, users, features, concepts, or knowledge.
  • Targeted model editing to revise learned knowledge or behavior after training.
  • Selective forgetting of inaccurate, outdated, harmful, or otherwise unwanted information.

Retention and Continual Learning

  • Balancing targeted changes with the preservation of useful knowledge and capabilities during model updates.
  • Continual and lifelong learning, including approaches to mitigating catastrophic forgetting and interference.
  • Efficient methods for controlling what persists and what changes across successive model updates.

Evaluation, Guarantees, and Limits

  • Definitions, metrics, and protocols for assessing intended changes, preserved capabilities, and unintended effects.
  • Auditing and verification of claims about privacy, robustness, deletion, editing, and retention.
  • Theoretical guarantees for controlling, modifying, or removing learned information, and the assumptions on which they depend.
  • Trade-offs, lower bounds, impossibility results, and failure modes of memorization control.
